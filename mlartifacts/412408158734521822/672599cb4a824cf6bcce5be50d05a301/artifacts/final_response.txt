General guidance for American nationals traveling abroad:
- Check the destination country's entry requirements on its official embassy or consulate website and on the U.S. Department of State travel site (travel.state.gov). These are the authoritative sources.
- Confirm whether a visa is required for U.S. citizens, and if so what type (tourist, business, transit, work, etc.), the application process (online vs. consulate), fees, and current processing times.
- Verify passport validity rules — many countries require at least 3–6 months of validity beyond your planned departure date.
- Check whether transit/stopover airports require a transit visa if you leave the secure area or change airports.
- Look into electronic authorizations (e.g., ESTA for travel to the U.S. under Visa Waiver, eTA/e-visa programs for other countries) and apply well before travel.
- Check health/vaccine and COVID-related entry rules and whether proof of insurance or testing is required.
- For returning to the United States: U.S. citizens do not need a visa to re-enter, but must have a valid U.S. passport (or appropriate documents for land/sea travel such as a passport card where accepted).
