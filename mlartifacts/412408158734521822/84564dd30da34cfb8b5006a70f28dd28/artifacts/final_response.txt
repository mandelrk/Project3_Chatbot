Here’s a clear summary of the baggage rules based on the information we have, plus practical guidance:

Key rules and carrier responsibilities
- Checked-bag handling: When you hand baggage to the airline for checking, the carrier takes custody and issues a baggage identification tag for each piece. If your bag has no name or ID, you must put one on it before the airline accepts it.
- Same-flight rule: Checked baggage is normally carried on the same aircraft as the passenger. If that is impracticable, the carrier will put the baggage on the next flight with available space.
- Refusal to carry: Airlines may refuse to accept items as checked baggage because of size, shape, weight, character, or improper packing. Special items (sports equipment, pets, oversized/overweight bags) may have different rules and usually require advance arrangements.
- Free allowance and excess fees: Carriers set a free baggage allowance in their own regulations. Bags over that allowance incur excess-baggage charges that you must pay according to the carrier’s rules. Some carriers offer excess-value declarations for higher-value contents for an extra charge.
- Security searches: For safety and security, the carrier may request or perform searches of passengers and baggage (even in the passenger’s absence if they are unavailable).
- Gate-checked/valet/specialty bags: These are treated as checked baggage for the purposes of fee/refund rules.

Delays and baggage fee refunds (consumer protections)
- If a checked bag is significantly delayed, the Department of Transportation requires airlines to refund baggage fees in defined circumstances. This includes gate-checked bags, valet bags, oversized/overweight bags, specialty checked bags (e.g., sporting equipment, pets), and bags that exceed normal allowance.
- If multiple bags are delayed and there is no unique identifier linking fees to individual bags, the airline may have to refund the highest per-bag fee on an escalated fee scale.
- For passengers on a paid baggage-fee subscription, the rule requires refunding an amount comparable to what another passenger of similar status (without subscription) would have paid for the delayed bag.
- The delay “clock” starts at the arrival of the flight and ends when the carrier delivers the checked bag. Airlines are required to make every reasonable effort to return mishandled baggage within the timeframes set by the rule (the specific delay threshold for a “significant” delay and exact timeframes are not provided in the materials we have).

What’s not fully specified here
- The exact definition/threshold of “significantly delayed” baggage and the precise timeframes for refunds are not included in the excerpt you provided. Also, specific size/weight limits, fee amounts, and packing rules are set by each airline in its own contract of carriage.

Practical steps and tips
- Before travel: Check your airline’s baggage policy (allowance, size/weight limits, fees, special-item rules) and label your checked bags with your name/contact. Pack valuables and essentials in carry-on.
- At check-in: Get and keep your baggage claim tags/receipts.
- If your bag is delayed: Report it immediately at the airline’s baggage service desk and obtain a report/reference number. Keep receipts for any essential purchases (to support claims). Ask the airline about baggage-fee refunds under their delay policy and any compensation you may be due.
- For specialty items or oversized/overweight baggage: Contact the airline in advance for rules and reservation requirements.
- If you need help: You can contact the airline’s customer service or file a complaint with the DOT if you believe your consumer rights were violated.
