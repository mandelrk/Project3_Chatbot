I don’t have each airline’s specific cancellation rules in the knowledge base you provided. What I can tell you from the Department of Transportation material is what airlines are required to disclose and the kinds of consumer protections you should look for:

Key protections carriers must disclose
- Airlines must clearly publish refund and rebooking policies (in their Customer Service Plans and on their websites/phone reservations). These policies must cover:
  - Consumers’ right to a refund when the airline cancels a flight or makes a significant schedule change.
  - When “automatic refunds” apply under certain circumstances.
  - Refunds and rebooking options when a consumer is non-responsive to the carrier’s offer of a significantly changed or alternate flight.
  - Refunding fees for ancillary services that were paid for but not provided (e.g., paid seat assignments, baggage fees if the service wasn’t delivered).
  - Refunds for significantly delayed or lost checked bags.
  - Special refund procedures for passengers with disabilities and for other people on the same reservation who do not want to continue travel without that passenger.

What the rule does NOT give in this excerpt
- The KB does not list specific time thresholds for what counts as a “significant” schedule change for every carrier. (The Department considered different standards and some carriers set their own standards—examples cited included 2, 3, 4 or 6 hours—so you must check the airline’s own policy for the exact threshold that applies.)
- The KB does not list airline-by-airline cancellation terms, timelines for processing refunds, or exact steps for submitting a claim.

What you can do next
- Check the airline’s website for its Customer Service Plan or Refund/Cancellation policy (look for “Customer Service Plan,” “Refund policy,” or “Flight disruption policy”).
- If you already have a booking, contact the airline directly or let us help: if you booked through Wanderlust Travels, we can pull your reservation details and assist with rebooking or filing a refund request.
- Keep all booking confirmations, receipts for ancillary purchases, and any communications from the airline—these will help if you file for a refund.
- If the situation involves a passenger with a disability, note that there are specific refund protections for that passenger and the other people on the same reservation.
