Yes. Under the DOT’s guidance, a practice is “deceptive” if it is likely to mislead a consumer acting reasonably about a material matter. A “material” matter is one that would likely affect the consumer’s decision or conduct (for example, whether a fee is charged or whether money is refundable). The DOT gives concrete examples — e.g., failing to disclose that a service fee is nonrefundable or failing to disclose the existence/amount of a refund fee — and treats those failures as deceptive. The DOT may investigate and take enforcement action (including civil penalties) for such deceptive practices.
