Here are the key points you should know about checked baggage when traveling to the United States, based on U.S. Department of Transportation rules and common carrier requirements:

What the DOT requires for delayed or lost checked bags
- Carriers must make every reasonable effort to return mishandled baggage within the timeframes set by the rule; if they don’t, they may have to refund any checked-baggage fees.  
- For international itineraries that include a nonstop segment to/from the U.S. of 12 hours or less, a bag is considered “significantly delayed” after 15 hours.  
- For international itineraries that include a nonstop segment to/from the U.S. longer than 12 hours, carriers are allowed up to 30 hours before they must refund the baggage fee.  
- The “baggage delay clock” starts at the flight’s arrival and ends when the carrier delivers the checked bag. If the carrier provides alternative transportation, the clock can start when you arrive at your destination using that alternative transport.  
- Exemptions: carriers are not required to refund fees when the delay is caused by certain passenger actions (for example, not picking up and rechecking a bag at the first U.S. international entry point as required by CBP) or other specified circumstances. Baggage fees included in the fare or provided as a complimentary benefit (frequent flyer or credit card perks) are generally not subject to refund under this rule.

Other practical baggage rules to know
- When you hand over checked baggage, the carrier issues a baggage identification tag and takes custody. Make sure your bag has your name/contact info attached before check-in.  
- Checked bags are normally carried on the same aircraft as you; if that is impracticable, the carrier will carry them on the next flight with space.  
- Free baggage allowance, excess-baggage charges, and rules for declaring excess value vary by airline—check your carrier’s regulations for exact size/weight limits and fees. Specialty items (oversized, pets, etc.) may be treated differently and can have different refund/exemption rules.

What you should do if your bag is delayed or lost
- Report the problem immediately at the airline’s baggage service desk at the airport and get a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) or similar written claim/record.  
- Keep the baggage claim tags, your boarding pass, and receipts for any essential purchases you make while the bag is missing.  
- Ask the airline about their timeline for returning the bag and about possible fee refunds if the bag becomes “significantly delayed” under the DOT timing rules.  
- If you bought a baggage-fee subscription, the airline must refund at least the lowest comparable baggage fee charged to a similar passenger without the subscription if your bag is significantly delayed or lost.
