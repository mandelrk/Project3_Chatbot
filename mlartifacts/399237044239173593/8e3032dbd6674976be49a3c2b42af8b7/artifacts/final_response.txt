Short answer: Sometimes — but only under specific conditions.

What the rule says (in plain terms)
- A ticket agent may keep a service charge that was for issuing the original ticket (a ticket‑issuance/service fee) even if the airline cancels the flight and a refund is due — because that fee is considered payment for a service already provided.  
- That retained fee must be on a per‑passenger basis and its existence, amount, and whether it is non‑refundable must have been clearly and prominently disclosed to you when you bought the ticket.  
- If a fee was not disclosed (or the disclosure was misleading), keeping that fee is likely a deceptive practice and the Department of Transportation would view it as improper.  
- Ticket agents must provide refunds in the original form of payment unless you agree to another cash‑equivalent form.

What you should do next
- Check your booking confirmation/receipt for any service‑fee disclosures (name of fee, amount, per‑passenger basis, and a statement that it is non‑refundable if applicable).  
- If the fee was clearly disclosed as a ticket‑issuance/service fee, the agent may legally retain it when issuing a refund.  
- If the fee was not clearly disclosed, ask the agent for a full refund breakdown and dispute any retained fee. If they refuse and the fee was not disclosed, you can file a complaint with the U.S. DOT’s Aviation Consumer Protection office.
